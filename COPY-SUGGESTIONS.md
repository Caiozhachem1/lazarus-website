# Lazarus Website — Copy Suggestions

A section-by-section breakdown of what's working, what could be sharper, and proposed alternatives. Nothing is final — let's use this to brainstorm.

---

## 1. Hero

**Current:**
> AI monitoring for your physical assets

> Detect intrusions, fires, theft, and safety violations in seconds — across mines, forests, industrial sites, and remote infrastructure.

**What's working:** The headline is clear and direct. The subtitle is specific and scannable — it tells you exactly what the product does and who it's for in one sentence. The HUD overlay (camera feed aesthetic) does a lot of the heavy lifting visually.

**Where it could be sharper:** "AI monitoring for your physical assets" is accurate but reads slightly generic — it could be any IoT monitoring company. The word "your" is conversational but not needed. Consider whether the headline should lead with the *outcome* (security/protection) rather than the *method* (AI monitoring).

**Option A — Keep it close:**
> AI monitoring for physical assets

(Drop "your" for a cleaner, more brand-level statement)

**Option B — Lead with outcome:**
> Every threat. Detected in seconds.

(Let the subtitle carry the "how" — AI, sensors, etc.)

**Option C — Lead with the problem:**
> Your perimeter is too big to patrol. We made it intelligent.

(More narrative, sets up the product)

**Option D — Bolder/punchier:**
> See everything. Miss nothing.

(Extremely short, punchy — relies on the subtitle and visuals to fill in context)

## 2. What We Do

**Current title:**
> We turn your perimeter into an intelligent, early-warning network

**What's working:** Clear value prop. The word "intelligent" does good work here.

**Suggestion:** The "We turn your..." construction is a bit passive. Consider:
- "Your perimeter, turned into an intelligent early-warning network" (reframes around the customer)
- "An intelligent early-warning network — built around your perimeter" (product-first)

### Feature cards

**Reduced losses** — "Seconds of response time can mean the difference between a minor incident and a catastrophic loss." This is two sentences saying one thing. Tighter version: "Contain threats in their earliest stages — before a minor incident becomes a catastrophic loss."

**Persistent awareness** — "without human fatigue" is a nice line but "day and night, in any weather" is slightly cliché for security. Could punch it up: "Day and night, any weather, zero fatigue." (shorter, punchier rhythm)

**Natural language search** — The example query is great. The lead-in "Query your entire event history like a conversation" is slightly wordy. Consider: "Query your event history conversationally." (Then let the example do the work.)

**Safety & Compliance** — "Automatically generate" → "Auto-generate" saves a word. "international OSH standards without manual oversight" → "OSH standards — no manual oversight needed" (more direct)

## 3. What We Detect

**Current title:** One system. Many missions.
**Current subtitle:** A unified sensing and AI platform that continuously learns your perimeter to detect, verify, and understand emerging threats in real time.

**What's working:** The title is great — short, memorable. The subtitle establishes the platform concept.

**Suggestion on subtitle:** "Unified" and "continuously learns" are good but the sentence is a bit long. Consider: "One sensing and AI platform that learns your perimeter — detecting, verifying, and classifying threats in real time."

### Threat cards

**Perimeter intrusion** — Good but the opening "Detect unauthorized access across remote perimeters" is generic. The second sentence is the differentiator (Lazarus learns your normal). Consider leading with that: "Lazarus learns what belongs on your perimeter — your vehicles, your people, your patterns — and alerts the moment something doesn't."

**Theft & tampering** — Solid. Minor: "Capture a clean evidence trail" could connect more directly: "...unauthorized removal or interference — with a clean evidence trail for investigations and claims." (one sentence instead of two)

**Fire & smoke** — "smouldering" is British spelling. If your audience is global/American, use "smoldering." Also "visible to the eye" → "visible to the human eye" (subtle but reinforces the AI vs. human angle).

**Operational accidents** — "Identify" is weaker than "Detect." And "in real time across your operations" is filler. Consider: "Detect vehicle collisions, structural failures, equipment malfunctions, and hazardous spills the moment they happen."

**PPE & safety compliance** — "Flag red-zone violations with auditable imagery" is strong. "aligned to international OSH standards" could be shortened to "aligned to OSH standards" since you already said "international" earlier on the page.

**Environmental monitoring** — "Generate compliance reports automatically from sensor data" — the "from sensor data" is implied. Consider: "Compliance reports generated automatically." (punchier ending)

## 4. Technology

**Current title:** Distributed sensor network

**What's working:** The feature list is strong. The network map visualization is excellent. The 01-04 numbering gives it a technical, systematic feel.

### Feature descriptions

**No infrastructure required** — "Our sensors are solar powered and use satellite and radio connectivity. We bring everything we need" — the "We bring everything we need" is conversational but slightly vague. Consider: "Solar-powered sensors with satellite and radio connectivity. No trenching, no towers, no grid dependency — minimal footprint."

**Advanced multi-modal sensing** — "We use AI to fuse multimodal sensor data in order to provide extremely reliable and early detection" is wordy ("in order to provide"). Consider: "AI fuses gas, acoustic, thermal, video, and more — delivering extremely reliable, early detection that no single sensor could achieve alone."

**Low maintenance** — This is the thinnest description. "Designed for long-term, low-maintenance operation in the harshest environments" is a single generic sentence. Consider adding specifics: "Built for years of unattended operation in extreme heat, dust, rain, and cold. No scheduled service visits required."

**Detection latency: seconds** — "Detect events, from fires to intrusions, seconds after they occur" could land harder with contrast: "From fires to intrusions, events are detected and classified within seconds of occurring — not minutes, not hours."

## 5. Industries

**Current title:** Protecting critical assets worldwide
**Current subtitle:** From remote mines to sprawling industrial complexes, Lazarus adapts to your environment.

**Suggestion on subtitle:** Could be more specific about the *range*: "From open-pit mines to coastal resorts, Lazarus adapts to your terrain, your threats, and your operations."

### Industry cards — mostly tight. A few tweaks:

- **Mining:** "Monitor" → "Secure" (stronger verb)
- **Forestry:** "Early wildfire detection" → "Wildfire detection minutes earlier" (more specific, implies the competitive advantage)
- **Industrial:** "accident prevention" → "real-time accident detection" (more accurate to what the product actually does)
- **Utilities:** Add "theft" to the threat list alongside "tampering and natural hazards" — it's a big one for utilities
- **Agribusiness:** "remote agricultural operations" is accurate but slightly thin. Could add "and expansive" to paint the scale picture

## 6. Stats

**Current:**
- Seconds → From event to alert
- Days → From contract to deployment
- 24/7 → Autonomous operation

**What's working:** Clean, punchy format.

**Concern:** "Seconds" and "Days" as the big stat numbers are creative but they're *words*, not *numbers*. Stats sections on sites like this typically have big numerals that pop visually. They also lack the immediate "wow" factor of a concrete figure.

**Option A — Make them numeric:**
- <30s → From event to alert
- 0 → Infrastructure required (the "$0" angle — nothing to build)
- 24/7 → Autonomous operation

**Option B — Keep words but add context:**
- Seconds → From event to alert (already strong)
- Days, not months → From contract to live deployment
- 24/7/365 → Autonomous, uninterrupted monitoring

**Option C — Totally different stats:**
If you have real deployment numbers, those would be more compelling than abstract claims. Things like "X sensors deployed," "Y km of perimeter monitored," "Z countries" etc.

## 7. About

**Current:**
> Built for the physical world

> We're a team of engineers, researchers, and operators building technology for the physical world. We design and build full stack — hardware, software, and AI. Pushing the boundaries of engineering to the limit.

> Our backgrounds span self-driving vehicles, autonomous drones, semiconductor research, and defense technology.

**What's working:** The backgrounds list is impressive and specific.

**Where it's thin:** This is the lightest section on the page. Two paragraphs and a "Join Our Team" button. For a company selling to enterprise/industrial buyers, the About section is where trust gets built. It currently reads more like a startup pitch than a company you'd trust with critical infrastructure.

**Suggestions:**
- The first paragraph has three sentences that each start a new thought. Consider combining: "We're engineers, researchers, and operators who build full-stack technology — hardware, software, and AI — for the most demanding environments on earth."
- The second paragraph could close with *why* you build in-house: "We design and manufacture in-house because the problems we solve don't have off-the-shelf answers."
- "Pushing the boundaries of engineering to the limit" is the weakest line — it's a generic tech tagline. Consider cutting it or replacing with something more specific to your mission.
- Consider adding a third paragraph about the company's mission or where you operate (geographically).

## 8. CTA / Contact

**Current:**
> Ready to secure your assets?

> Get in touch with our team to schedule a demo and learn how Lazarus can protect your operations.

**What's working:** The form is clean. Having both name + email + company + textarea is right for a B2B demo request.

**Suggestion:** "Ready to secure your assets?" is a fine CTA but it's asking a yes/no question. A statement or invitation can feel stronger:
- "See Lazarus in action" (direct, action-oriented)
- "Let's design your perimeter" (collaborative, specific)
- "Schedule a deployment assessment" (more enterprise/consultative)

The description could be more specific about what happens next: "Tell us about your site and we'll show you exactly how Lazarus would deploy, detect, and protect — tailored to your operations."

## 9. Meta Tags / SEO

**Current title:** Lazarus Sensors — AI Monitoring for Physical Assets
**Current description:** Lazarus turns your perimeter into an intelligent, early-warning network...

**Suggestion:** The meta description should lead with the clearest value prop and include key terms buyers search for. Consider: "AI-powered monitoring for physical assets. Detect intrusions, fires, theft, and safety violations in seconds across mines, forests, industrial sites, and remote infrastructure."

OG description could be more action-oriented: "Deploy AI-powered sensors that detect intrusions, fires, theft, and safety violations in seconds — no infrastructure required."

## 10. Global Notes

- **Spelling:** "smouldering" (British) vs "smoldering" (American) — pick one and be consistent. Recommend American if your primary market is the Americas.
- **Tone:** Confident and technical throughout — this is good. The site avoids jargon while still feeling credible.
- **Repetition:** "real time" appears in multiple sections. It's a key selling point so the repetition is strategic, but worth tracking to make sure it doesn't feel redundant.
- **"We" vs product-first language:** The tech section uses a lot of "We use... We capture... We bring..." Consider shifting some to product-first ("Lazarus fuses..." / "Our sensors...") to keep the focus on the product rather than the team.
- **Footer tagline:** Currently "AI monitoring for your physical assets." — mirrors the hero. This is fine but could be a standalone brand line that's slightly different, like "Intelligent security for the physical world."
